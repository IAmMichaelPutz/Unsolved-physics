6 Falling Rod

A massless rod of length l has a point mass m attached to one end. The rod stands on a frictionless table. For small deflections from the vertical position, the rod falls due to gravity.

i) Set up the equation of motion using the Lagrange function.

ii) Solve the equation of motion for the initial conditions

\[ \phi(0) = \phi_0, \dot{\phi}(0) = 0 \tag{1} \]

using the small angle approximation.

Show Solution

Part 1: Setting up the equation of motion

As generalized coordinate, we use the angle φ of the rod to the vertical. Therefore, for the kinetic and potential energy in planar polar coordinates:

\begin{align} T &= \frac{m}{2}l^2\dot{\phi}^2 \tag{2} \\ V &= mgl\cos\phi \tag{3} \\ \mathcal{L} &= \frac{m}{2}l^2\dot{\phi}^2 - mgl\cos\phi \tag{4} \end{align}

Using the Euler-Lagrange equation, we get the equation of motion:

\[ ml^2\ddot{\phi} = mgl\sin\phi \tag{5} \]

Part 2: Small angle approximation

In the small angle approximation, the equation of motion takes the following form:

\[ \ddot{\phi} = \frac{g}{l}\phi \tag{6} \]

We make the ansatz φ(t) = C exp(λt) to solve the initial value problem. Substitution yields the characteristic equation for λ:

\[ \lambda^2 - \frac{g}{l} = 0 \to \lambda_{1,2} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} \tag{7} \]
\[ \phi(t) = C_1e^{\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}}t} + C_2e^{-\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}}t} \tag{8} \]

If we substitute the initial conditions and use the definition \(\cosh(x) = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}\), we get as solution:

\[ \phi(t) = \phi_0\cosh\left(\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}}t\right) \tag{9} \]
