2 Molecular Vibrations (Exam Problem)

A diatomic molecule can perform vibrations as well as rotational movements. For simplicity, only movements in a fixed plane shall be considered. The potential is given by \(U(r) = \frac{1}{2}\omega_0^2(r-r_0)^2\), where \(\mu = \frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}\) is the reduced mass, r the relative distance, and r₀ the equilibrium distance for x = 0.

i) Show that in an inertial system where the center of mass rests at the origin, the molecule is described by the following Lagrange function:

\[ \mathcal{L} = \frac{\mu}{2}(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\varphi}^2) - U(r) \tag{1} \]

Only rotations and vibrations in this plane are considered.

Show Solution

First, we need to consider which generalized coordinates to use. The geometry of the problem offers φ and r. To express the position vectors of mass points 1 and 2 in φ and r, we use the center of mass (problem statement), i.e.:

\begin{align} \mathbf{S} &= \frac{m_1\mathbf{r_1} + m_2\mathbf{r_2}}{m_1 + m_2} = 0 \Rightarrow m_1\mathbf{r_1} + m_2\mathbf{r_2} = 0 \tag{2} \\ \mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{r_1} - \mathbf{r_2} \tag{3} \end{align}

After brief calculation one finds:

\begin{align} \mathbf{r_1} &= \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\mathbf{r} \tag{4} \\ \mathbf{r_2} &= -\frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\mathbf{r} \tag{5} \end{align}

For the kinetic energy:

\begin{align} T &= \frac{m_1}{2}\dot{\mathbf{r_1}}^2 + \frac{m_2}{2}\dot{\mathbf{r_2}}^2 \tag{6} \\ &= \frac{m_1^2}{2(m_1 + m_2)^2}\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 + \frac{m_2^2}{2(m_1 + m_2)^2}\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 \tag{7} \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 = \frac{\mu}{2}\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2 \tag{8} \end{align}

Now introducing the plane polar coordinates φ and r (see lecture) gives:

\[ T = \frac{\mu}{2}(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\varphi}^2) \tag{9} \]

For the Lagrange function follows:

\[ \mathcal{L} = \frac{\mu}{2}(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\varphi}^2) - U(r) \tag{10} \]

ii) State 2 conserved quantities with justification.

Solution:

Looking at the Lagrange function more closely, one sees that \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\varphi} = 0\). Thus φ is a cyclic coordinate and from

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\varphi}} = 0 \tag{11} \]

follows the conserved quantity:

\[ \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\varphi}} = const. = \mu r^2\dot{\varphi} =: l \tag{12} \]

l corresponds to the magnitude of angular momentum. The total energy E = T + U is also conserved since the Lagrange function is not explicitly time-dependent.

iii) Set up the equation of motion and simplify it. Express the equation for radial motion through the distance ρ = r - r₀ from equilibrium position.

Solution:

The Euler-Lagrange equation for the radial component \(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{r}} - \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial r} = 0\) gives:

\begin{align} \frac{d}{dt}\mu\dot{r} &= \mu r\dot{\varphi}^2 - \mu\omega_0^2(r-r_0) \tag{13} \\ \mu\ddot{r} - \mu r\dot{\varphi}^2 + \mu\omega_0^2(r-r_0) &= 0 \tag{14} \end{align}

With the relation \(\mu r^2\dot{\varphi} = l\):

\[ \mu\ddot{r} - \mu r\frac{l^2}{\mu^2r^4} + \mu\omega_0^2(r-r_0) = 0 \tag{15} \]

With r - r₀ = ρ and \(\dot{r} = \dot{\rho}\) follows the equation of motion:

\[ \ddot{\rho} - \frac{l^2}{\mu^2(\rho+r_0)^3} + \omega_0^2\rho = 0 \tag{16} \]
