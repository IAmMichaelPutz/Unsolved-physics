4. Bead on a Helix (**)

A bead of mass m slides frictionlessly on a helix with radius R and a > 0:

\[ \mathbf{r}(t) = \begin{pmatrix} R\cos\phi(t) \\ R\sin\phi(t) \\ a\phi(t) \end{pmatrix} \]

Gravity acts in negative z-direction.

a) What are the constraints?

b) Formulate the first-kind Lagrange equations.

c) Use the constraints to simplify the equations of motion and determine the constraint forces.

Hint: Use cylindrical coordinates (ρ, φ, z).

Show Solution

a) Constraints:

\begin{align} f_1 &= \rho - R = 0 \tag{1} \\ f_2 &= z - a\phi = 0 \tag{2} \end{align}

where a is a measure of the helix pitch.

b) First-kind Lagrange Equations:

\begin{align} L(\rho,\phi,z,\dot{\rho},\dot{\phi},\dot{z}) &= T - U = \frac{1}{2}m(\dot{\rho}^2 + \rho^2\dot{\phi}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - mgz \tag{3} \\[2ex] m\ddot{\rho} - m\rho\dot{\phi}^2 &= \sum_{k=1}^2\lambda_k\frac{\partial f_k}{\partial\rho} = \lambda_1 = Z_\rho \tag{4} \\[2ex] m(\rho^2\ddot{\phi} + 2\rho\dot{\rho}\dot{\phi}) &= \sum_{k=1}^2\lambda_k\frac{\partial f_k}{\partial\phi} = -a\lambda_2 = Z_\phi \tag{5} \\[2ex] m\ddot{z} + mg &= \sum_{k=1}^2\frac{\partial f_k}{\partial z} = \lambda_2 = Z_z \tag{6} \end{align}

c) Simplified Equations:

Using ρ = R ⇒ ρ̇ = ρ̈ = 0 and z = aφ:

\begin{align} -mR\dot{\phi}^2 &= \lambda_1 \tag{7} \\ mR^2\ddot{\phi} &= -a\lambda_2 \tag{8} \\ ma\ddot{\phi} + mg &= \lambda_2 \tag{9} \end{align}

For λ₂:

\[ \lambda_2 = \frac{mgR^2}{a^2 + R^2} = Z_z = -\frac{1}{a}Z_\phi \tag{10} \]

Using this result:

\begin{align} \ddot{\phi} &= -\frac{ag}{a^2 + R^2} \tag{11} \\ \dot{\phi} &= -\frac{ag}{a^2 + R^2}t \tag{12} \\ \lambda_1 &= -\frac{mRa^2g^2}{(a^2 + R^2)^2}t^2 = Z_\rho \tag{13} \end{align}

5. Variational Principle (**)

a) Consider a Lagrangian L(q,q̇,q̈;t) that also depends on the second derivative of q with respect to time. Show that the Euler-Lagrange equation for this function is:

\[ \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{q}} + \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\ddot{q}}\right)L(q,\dot{q},\ddot{q};t) = 0 \]

where we assume that neither q nor q̇ are varied at the endpoints, i.e., δq(t₁) = δq(t₂) = δq̇(t₁) = δq̇(t₂) = 0.

b) Show that the shortest connection between two points (geodesic line) on a sphere is given by a great circle arc.

Show Solution

a) Variation of the Action:

\begin{align} \delta S[q] &= 0 \tag{1} \\ \Leftrightarrow \frac{d}{d\eta}S(\eta)\bigg|_{\eta=0} &= 0 \tag{2} \end{align}

After applying calculus of variations and integrating by parts twice:

\[ \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}} + \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{q}}\right)\delta q(t)dt = 0 \tag{3} \]

b) Geodesic on Sphere:

Line element on sphere of radius R:

\[ ds = R\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\vartheta\phi'^2}d\vartheta \tag{4} \]

Lagrangian:

\[ L(\phi,\phi';\vartheta) = \sqrt{1 + \sin^2\vartheta\phi'^2} \tag{5} \]

Since L is independent of φ, φ is cyclic coordinate and:

\[ p_\phi = \frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi'} = \frac{\sin^2\vartheta\phi'}{\sqrt{1 + \sin^2\vartheta\phi'^2}} = const \tag{6} \]

For first point at North Pole (ϑ = 0), p_φ = 0, therefore φ' = 0 and φ = const.

Therefore, the geodesics are great circle arcs.

6. Tit For Tat (***)

Two point-like bodies of equal mass m move in Earth's homogeneous gravitational field frictionlessly on a vertical and a 45° inclined line respectively. They are connected by an ideal spring with spring constant k, which has length l = 0 when relaxed. No other forces act.

a) Set up the Lagrangian in variables y₁ and y₂, the vertical components of the mass point coordinates.

b) Derive the equations of motion.

c) Determine the equilibrium position.

d) Show that with new coordinates ξ₁ and ξ₂ describing displacement from equilibrium, the Lagrangian simplifies to:

\[ L = \frac{m}{2}(2\dot{\xi}_1^2 + \dot{\xi}_2^2) - \frac{k}{2}(2\xi_1^2 + \xi_2^2 - 2\xi_1\xi_2) \]
Show Solution

a) Lagrangian:

\begin{align} T &= \frac{1}{2}m(2\dot{y}_1^2 + \dot{y}_2^2) \tag{1} \\ U &= -mg(y_1 + y_2) + \frac{1}{2}k[y_1^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2] \tag{2} \\ L &= \frac{1}{2}m(2\dot{y}_1^2 + \dot{y}_2^2) + mg(y_1 + y_2) - \frac{1}{2}k(2y_1^2 + y_2^2 - 2y_1y_2) \tag{3} \end{align}

b) Equations of Motion:

\begin{align} \ddot{y}_1 &= \frac{g}{2} - \frac{k}{2m}(2y_1 - y_2) \tag{4} \\ \ddot{y}_2 &= g - \frac{k}{m}(y_2 - y_1) \tag{5} \end{align}

c) Equilibrium Position:

\begin{align} 2y_1 - y_2 &= \frac{mg}{k} =: y_0 \tag{6} \\ y_2 - y_1 &= \frac{mg}{k} = y_0 \tag{7} \end{align}

Therefore:

\begin{align} y_2 &= \frac{3}{2}y_1 \tag{8} \\ y_1^{(0)} &= 2y_0 \tag{9} \\ y_2^{(0)} &= 3y_0 \tag{10} \end{align}

d) New Coordinates:

\begin{align} \xi_1 &= y_1 - y_1^{(0)} = y_1 - 2y_0 \tag{11} \\ \xi_2 &= y_2 - y_2^{(0)} = y_2 - 3y_0 \tag{12} \end{align}

Substituting these into the Lagrangian and simplifying yields the required form.
