5 Rotating Mass Point

Consider a massless ring rotating with constant angular velocity ω in Earth's gravitational field, on which a mass m can slide frictionlessly.

i) Set up the Lagrange function and determine a conserved quantity.

ii) Determine the equilibrium position θ and show that it can be different from 0.

Show Solution

Part 1: Lagrange Function and Conservation Law

The z-axis points downward and the 0-point is the center of the ring. For the Lagrange function in general:

\[ \mathcal{L} = T - U = \frac{m}{2}(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2) - (mgz) \tag{1} \]

Therefore in spherical coordinates (see lecture):

\[ \mathcal{L} = \frac{m}{2}(r^2\dot{\theta}^2 + r^2\dot{\varphi}^2\sin^2\theta) + mgr\cos\theta \tag{2} \]

Note: r and \(\dot{\varphi} = \omega\) are constants.

The Euler-Lagrange equations for the angle θ gives:

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\theta}} - \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\theta} \]
\[ \to mr^2\ddot{\theta} = mr^2\omega^2\sin\theta\cos\theta - mgr\sin\theta \tag{3} \]

Since \(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\varphi} = 0\), φ is a cyclic coordinate and we get the conserved quantity (angular momentum conservation):

\[ \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\varphi}} = const. = mr^2\sin^2\theta\dot{\varphi} =: l(= L_z) \tag{4} \]

Part 2: Equilibrium Position

In equilibrium position, \(\ddot{\theta} = 0\), i.e., the angle θ does not change with time. Taking the equation of motion (3) and setting \(\ddot{\theta} = 0\), we get:

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{g}{r\omega^2} \tag{5} \]

Case distinction:

\begin{align} \text{If }\frac{g}{r\omega^2} &> 1, \text{ thus }\frac{g}{r} > \omega^2 \text{ then the only solution is }\theta = 0 \tag{6} \\ \text{If }\frac{g}{r\omega^2} &< 1, \text{ thus }\frac{g}{r} < \omega^2 \text{ then we get both solutions: }\theta = \pm \arccos(\frac{g}{r\omega^2}) \tag{7} \end{align}
