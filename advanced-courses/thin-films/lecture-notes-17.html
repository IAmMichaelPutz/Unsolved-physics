2.6 Pressure Gauges

Use different properties of (diluted) gases to gain a measure of the pressure inside vacuum system

Most Common Gauges:

  • Diaphragm gauge: utilize change in force/capacitance
  • Pirani gauge: wire heated to constant temperature
    Surrounding gas will cool the wire due to convection
  • Ionization gauge: emitted electrons ionize residual gas molecules
    \[ \text{acceleration to an electrode detects pressure-dependent current} \]

4.7 Vacuum Leaks

No vacuum can be fully sealed!

Typical Leaks:

  1. Out gassing: pumps, equipment, samples
  2. Flange based/gaskets (unavoidable to some degree, never completely)

Leakage rate can be easily measured by the time-dependent pressure rise after disconnecting the pump.

3. Thin Film Deposition

Today's deposition techniques can be roughly divided into two groups:

  1. Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD)
  2. Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)

PVD:

  • Relies on solid or liquid sources and uses physical mechanisms to transfer atoms into the gas phase and towards the substrate
  • Low/very low pressure needed
  • Mostly no chemical reactions during the growth process
  • Mostly restricted to small substrates/areas

CVD:

  • Relies on gaseous precursors
  • Atoms within precursors are transferred to substrate by chemical reactions
  • (High-temperature) and high-pressure environments
  • Coverage of large areas and complex shapes of substrates
