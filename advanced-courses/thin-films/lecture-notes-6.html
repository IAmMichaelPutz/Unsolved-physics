1.5 Surface Tension + Surface Stress

⇒ Now we move from infinite solids to surface and ambient conditions

Pulling on infinitely extended slab with cleavage area

Solid will not cleave spontaneously, additional energy needed

$$E = \frac{\gamma \cdot A}{\text{surface tension}}$$
Energy required for surface creation

⇒ Internal energy U changes!

$$U = TS - pV + \mu N + \gamma A \quad (*)$$
Fundamental thermodynamic relation

In equilibrium, the semi-infinite solid always co-exists with its vapor

Interface diagram with solid and vapor phases

At the surface, atoms will move away from their original lattice sites to compensate for new conditions

⇒ Distortion $\epsilon_{ij}$

$$U = U(S, V, N, A\epsilon_{ij})$$
Internal energy as function of entropy, volume, particle number, and distortion
$$I \quad dU = \frac{\partial U}{\partial S}dS + \frac{\partial U}{\partial V}dV + \frac{\partial U}{\partial N}dN + A\frac{\partial U}{\partial \epsilon_{ij}}d\epsilon_{ij}$$
Differential form with $\frac{\partial U}{\partial \epsilon_{ij}}$ = surface stress $\sigma_{ij}$
$$\sigma_{ij} = c_{ijkl}\epsilon_{kl}$$
Hooke's Law
