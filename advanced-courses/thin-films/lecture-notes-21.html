3.1.3 Evaporation of Multi-Element Materials

Pure metals evaporate as single atoms

→ not the case for compounds! Compounds may/will undergo molecular changes → decomposition of constituents with different vapor pressures!

Ideal Case: Solutions of Two Elements

Elements A and B have identical interactions A-A, A-B, B-B

Raoult's Law:

Vapor pressure $p_B$ of element B in solution of material A will be reduced relative to that of pure B $(p_{B,0})$ in proportion to its mole fraction $\chi_B$ in the solution:

$p_B = \chi_B p_{B,0}$

In reality: interaction between A-B will differ from A-A

→ introduce $\gamma_B$ as phenomenological factor

$p_B = \gamma_B \cdot \chi_B \cdot p_{B,0}$
$\gamma_B \chi_B$ = activity

Example: Al-Cu thin film with 2 wt% Cu @ 1,300K

$\frac{\Gamma_{Al}}{\Gamma_{Cu}} = \frac{\phi_{Al}}{\phi_{Cu}} \cdot \frac{\Pi_{Al}}{\Pi_{Cu}} = \frac{98}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{M_{Cu}}{M_{Al}}}$

Approximate values:

  • $\gamma_{Al} = \gamma_{Cu} = 1$
  • $\Pi_{Al} = 27_a$
  • $\Pi_{Cu} = 63.5_u$
  • Vapor pressures: $\frac{p_{Al,0}}{p_{Cu,0}} = \frac{10^{-3}}{2\cdot10^{-11}}$
$\frac{\chi_{Al}}{\chi_{Cu}} \approx 15$ → mole fraction for 2 wt% Al-Cu Film

Note: This mole fraction only holds for very first moment, the stoichiometry will change due to different vapor pressures!

→ can be used to get graded/gradient films of various compositions!
