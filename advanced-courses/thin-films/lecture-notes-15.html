Lecture 5: Conductance and Pumping Speed

Conductance of a Pipe

\[q = \Phi \cdot B \cdot L = \frac{n}{4} v_{rms} \cdot B \cdot L\]
Number of particles striking the wall per unit time
\[\dot{q} = q \cdot mv = \frac{n}{4} v_{rms} \cdot BL \cdot mv\]
Momentum transferred to wall

Equilibrium Conditions

\[C = \frac{8}{3\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2k_BT}{m}} \cdot \frac{D^3}{L}\]
Pipe conductance formula

Lessons to be Learned:

  • For high gas flow, make conductors (pipe) wide and short
  • Ducts can act as (open) valves between chambers of different pressures

Pumping Speed

\[S = \frac{Q}{P}\]
Definition of pumping speed
\[S = \frac{C \cdot S_p}{C + S_p}\]
Effective pumping speed
S never exceeds Sp or C

Vacuum Pumps

Two general classes:

  • Gas transfer: removes gas molecules from the chamber permanently
  • Gas entrapment: particles are condensed or chemically/physically bound to surfaces inside the pump

Examples:

  • Roughing pumps (lubricated)
  • Oil-free pumps: often used as backup pumps in large systems or contamination-sensitive applications
  • Turbo molecular pump (for lower pressures)
