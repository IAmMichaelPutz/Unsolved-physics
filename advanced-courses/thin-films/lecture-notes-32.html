4.5 Sputtering of Alloys

Remember: evaporation of alloys is complicated as the material tends to fractionize and change composition.

Now with sputtering: in alloys every component is sputtered with a different yield!

Big difference: sputter target always remains solid!
→ no intermixing

Example: Binary alloy containing atom species A, B

At the surface:

\[n_A \text{ atoms of species A}\] \[n_B \text{ atoms of species B}\] \[n_A + n_B = n \text{ total number (density) of surface atoms}\]

Initial concentration:

\[C_A = \frac{n_A}{n}, C_B = \frac{n_B}{n}\] \[\text{(weighting factors e.g. 20\% vs. 80\% Fe}_{20}\text{Ni}_{80}\text{)}\]
Target maintenance is very important!

4.7 Plasma Chemistry and Etching

So far: gas only considered inert, even in its plasma state

But: even if working gas is entirely inert in equilibrium outside of plasma state, this is not necessarily the case in the plasma.
