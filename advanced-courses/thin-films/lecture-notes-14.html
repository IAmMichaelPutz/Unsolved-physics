2.3 Conductance

Assume we are in the molecular flow regime!

Consider gas flow Q through an opening (orifice) of area A, separating areas/chambers of pressure P₁ and P₂

The orifice is assumed to be 2D (not extended along gas flow direction)

\[ Q \sim \Delta P = |P_2 - P_1| \] \[ Q = C \cdot |P_2-P_1| \]

C: conductance

\[ \text{[C]} = \frac{\text{liters}}{\text{s}} \] \[ \text{[R]} = \frac{P_a \cdot \text{[C]}}{\text{[s]}} = \frac{j}{s} \]

C depends on:

Molecular Flow:

  • Geometry of the duct
  • Mass of the gas molecule
  • Temperature of gas

Viscous Flow Regime:

  • Viscosity
  • Pressure
  • Reynolds's number

C is much lower in molecular flow regime!

Orifice Conductance Calculation

\[ \phi = \frac{P}{\sqrt{2\pi m k_B T}} \] \[ \frac{dN}{dt} = (\phi_2 - \phi_1) \cdot A \] \[ Q = \frac{d}{dt}(PV) = \frac{d}{dt}(NkT) = \frac{dN}{dt}k_BT \] \[ Q = A\sqrt{\frac{k_B T}{2\pi m}} \Delta P \]
\[ C_0 \sim A\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} \] \[ C_0 \sim A\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} \sim D^2 \]

Orifice conductance

At room temperature:

\[ C \sim 11.7 \cdot A \left[\frac{l}{s}\right] \]
