3.1.1a Mathematical Interlude: Maxwell-Relations

We know: $dU = TdS - pdV$

$(dS, dV)$ pair of variables

We can only have pairs, otherwise the thermodynamic state would not be well-defined!

Alternative pairs: $(dU, dT)$, $(dS, dP)$, $(dP, dT)$

$dU = TdS - pdV$
$d(TS) = SdT + TdS$
$\Rightarrow dU = d(TS) - SdT - pdV$
$d(U-TS) = -SdT - pdV$
Helmholtz Free Energy
$d(U+pV) = TdS + Vdp$
Enthalpy
$d(U-TS+pV) = -SdT + Vdp$
Gibb's Free Energy

Using second derivative:

$\frac{\partial}{\partial V}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}\right) = \frac{\partial(-S)}{\partial V} = \frac{\partial^2F}{\partial V\partial T} = \frac{\partial^2F}{\partial T\partial V} = \frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial V}\right) = \frac{\partial(-p)}{\partial T}$
Maxwell Relation
$\Rightarrow \frac{\partial S}{\partial V} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}$

3.1.2 Vapor Pressure of Elements

Driving force of evaporation: change in enthalpy $S(V,T)$

Phase transition p(T) graph
$dS = \frac{\partial S}{\partial V}dV + \frac{\partial S}{\partial T}dT$
$dS = \frac{\partial S}{\partial V}dV = \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}dV$
During phase change due to T = const.
$\frac{dp}{dT} = \frac{\Delta H_{vap}}{T\cdot \Delta V}$
Clausius-Clapeyron Equation

Now let's consider a solid→vapor transition:

$\Delta V = V_2 - V_1 \approx V_2$ (from ideal gas: $pV_2 = RT$)

$p(T) = p_0 \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta H_{vap}}{RT}\right)$
Vapor Pressure Equation

Rule of thumb: if a material does not reach a vapor pressure ≥ 10⁻³ torr at its melting point, it will not evaporate sufficiently (in its solid state) [for a deposition]
