Example: Anisotropy of Surface Tension

Surface tension is in most cases depending on crystallographic orientation of sample surface

Two dimensional solid with angle θ and terrace width n·a
$$\text{if n is large:}$$ $$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{an}\right) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)$$ $$\approx \frac{1}{n}$$
Angular relation for large terrace widths
$$\text{Surface tension:} \quad \gamma(\theta) = \gamma(0) + \frac{\beta}{a}|\theta|$$
Lattice constant

This example demonstrates how surface tension varies with the crystallographic orientation, particularly for surfaces with terraces and steps. The relation shows a linear dependence on the miscut angle θ for small angles.

Key Points:

  • Surface tension depends on crystallographic orientation
  • For stepped surfaces, tension increases linearly with miscut angle
  • β represents step energy per unit length
