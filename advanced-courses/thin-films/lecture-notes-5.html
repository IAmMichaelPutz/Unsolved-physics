Example: Is it feasible to apply pure Al contacts on SiO₂ or should we rather use Cu?

⟶ 3 oxides: Al₂O₃, SiO₂, Cu₂O

$$\text{Si} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{SiO}_2 \qquad @\, 600°\text{C} \rightarrow \Delta G° = -720\, \frac{kJ}{mol}$$ $$4\text{Cu} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Cu}_2\text{O} \qquad\qquad \rightarrow \Delta G° = -225\, \frac{kJ}{mol}$$ $$\frac{4}{3}\text{Al} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \frac{2}{3}\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 \qquad \rightarrow \Delta G° = -925\, \frac{kJ}{mol}$$
$$\frac{4}{3}\text{Al} + \text{SiO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \frac{2}{3}\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{Si} + \text{O}_2$$ $$\Delta G = (-925\, \frac{kJ}{mol}) - (-720\, \frac{kJ}{mol}) = -205\, \frac{kJ}{mol}$$
$$4\text{Cu} + \text{SiO}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Cu}_2\text{O} + \text{Si}$$ $$\Delta G = -935\, \frac{kJ}{mol}$$

⇒ Al will reduce the device and introduce elementary Si (semiconductor), Cu will not
