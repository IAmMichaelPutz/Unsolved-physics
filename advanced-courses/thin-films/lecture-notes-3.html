1.3 Thermodynamics of Solids and Surfaces

In equilibrium, a one-component system of non-interacting particles is characterized completely by its internal energy U

$$ U = U(S, V, N) $$
where S: entropy, V: volume, N: particle number
$$ dU = \left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial S}\right|_{V,N} dS + \left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right|_{S,N} dV + \left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial N}\right|_{S,V} dN $$
$\left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial S}\right|_{V,N} = T$ (Temperature)
$\left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right|_{S,N} = -P$ (Pressure)
$\left.\frac{\partial U}{\partial N}\right|_{S,V} = \mu$ (Chemical potential)
$$ dU = TdS - PdV + \mu dN $$

Euler Equation:

$$ U = TS - PV + \mu N $$
$$ G = \mu N $$
G: Gibbs's (free) Energy

G = max. amount of non-expansion work that could be extracted from a closed system
