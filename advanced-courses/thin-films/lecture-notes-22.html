3.1.3 Maintaining Melt Stoichiometry

Option A: Increase Melt Volume

If melt volume is large compared to the mass of evaporated material, changes in stoichiometry are small

Option B: Add Material to Melt

Feed, e.g., a solid alloy wire of desired composition or of the missing element at a constant volumetric rate to maintain vapor flux ratio

Option C: Use Different Sources

e.g. in molecular beam epitaxy (MBE)

However: rather complicated approach, needs many sources, power supplies, shutters, etc. + UHV conditions!

Advantage: really high quality films

Option D: Fast Evaporation

Do evaporation really fast with a lot of energy

e.g. Flash evaporation

Idea: use enough power to sublimate the material rapidly => no time for decomposition (PLD)

3.1.4 Film Thickness Uniformity

How does deposited thickness scale with distance from the source?

Simple Model: Free-floating Point Source

Evaporant particles are imagined to originate from a small region dAe of a spherical source of surface area Ae

\[ \frac{dM_s}{dA_s} = \frac{M_e}{4\pi r^2} \cos\theta \]
Material deposition rate equation

For a surface source:

\[ \frac{d}{d_0} = \left[1 + \left(\frac{x}{h}\right)^2\right]^{-\frac{n+1}{2}} \]

Alternatives for Improving Uniformity:

  • Multiple substrates
  • Multiple sources
  • Source geometry optimization
  • Moving/rotating substrate with respect to source during evaporation
