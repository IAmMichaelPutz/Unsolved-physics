4.3 Electron Confinement by Magnetic Fields

Force on the Electron

\[ \vec{F}_L = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \]

If \(\vec{v} \perp \vec{B}\):

  • Velocity component ⊥ \(\vec{B}\)
  • Forces electrons on circular path
\[ F_C = \frac{m}{r}v_\perp^2 = \frac{m}{r}(v\sin\theta)^2 \] \[ F_L = q \cdot \frac{v\sin\theta}{v_\perp} \cdot B \] \[ \Rightarrow r = \frac{mv\sin\theta}{qB} \]

\(\vec{B} \perp \vec{E}\) - Cycloidal motion of electrons with frequency:

\[ \omega_c = \frac{qB}{m} \]
With the help of magnetic field, we can define a new cathode/anode geometry to keep electrons confined for sufficient times → wrap path into a circle!

It becomes possible to build the entire sputtering system into one device → sputter gun

Sputtering Limitations

Notes:
  • If incident particle energy is too low → no momentum transfer to target atoms → no sputtering
  • If incident particle energy is too high → implantation

Key Points

  • Electrodes now do not need to be conductors
  • Ions cannot follow fully and stay approx. at their positions
  • Energy only accelerates electrons for plasma generation
Note: Surfaces at floating potentials still charge negatively! Insulating targets therefore self-bias to negative potential → behaves like DC target for ions!
