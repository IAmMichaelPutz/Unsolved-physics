3.2.4 Pulsed Laser Deposition

Method: Laser ablation instead of constant electron beam to avoid charging and material decomposition

⇒ especially used for oxides

Note: oxygen-based films tend to grow oxygen-deficient

Solution: rapid ablation of the source material in high-oxygen atmosphere by high-energy laser

Typical pulse energies: 1-2J/cm², δt = 10ns

\[\text{Power density:} \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} = P \approx 100\ \frac{MW}{cm^2}\]

Laser penetration depth: 10-100nm

\[\Delta T \approx 10,000 - 20,000\ K\] \[v/v_s \approx 10,000\ m/s\]

Plume is highly directional, cos²⁰ϕ, n ≥ 12

Example: garnets

4. Sputtering

Why: alternative to thermal evaporation to avoid melts/composition variation

Idea: remove atoms from surfaces of solids at room temperature with the help of energetic ions

Working principle:

⇒ DC sputtering:

  • Target (source material) → cathode
  • Substrate holder → anode
  • Form a parallel plate capacitor
  • Cathode connected to neg. voltage terminal of DC power supply (kV)
  • Anode is grounded

After evacuation, a low pressure working gas (typically argon) is introduced at 1-100 mTorr

⇒ plasma formation/glow discharge

⇒ sputtering of target atoms by ion impingement

⇒ sputtered target material is deposited on substrate

⇒ more complex environment than evaporation
