4.2 AC / HF / RF Sputtering

Up to now: we can only deal with conducting targets! But we want insulators too...

Analysis of Insulator Sputtering

How much voltage would we need to force sputtering on insulator?

Example: quartz (SiO₂)

\[ \rho \approx 10^{16} \, \Omega\text{cm} \text{ (resistivity)} \]

Assumptions:

  • Thickness: t ~ 1mm
  • Typical current density: j ~ 1mA/cm²
\[ j = \sigma E = \sigma \frac{U_{DC}}{t} \] \[ U_{DC} = j\rho t = 10^{12}V \]
Insulators cannot technically be sputtered in DC mode!

In other words: only materials with \(\rho \lesssim 10^5 \, \Omega\text{cm}\) (\(U_{DC} \sim 100-1000V\)) can be sputtered in DC configuration

AC Sputtering Technique

New technique: use high frequency to cause electrons to oscillate

  • Electrons gain enough energy to ionize working gas
  • Ions should still accelerate to target

Frequency Considerations

  • At low frequencies (< 1MHz):
    • Ions are also sufficiently mobile
    • Sputtering at target and substrate → not wanted!
  • Above 1MHz:
    • Electrons gain enough energy to ionize within short acceleration period
    • No secondary electrons needed!

Sputter Gun Design

Important: To effectively couple electric power into the plasma through the insulating target, the AC source has to be impedance matched to the target plasma system (otherwise significant power is reflected!)

4.4 Sputtering Yield

\[ S = \frac{\text{Number of Sputtered Particles}}{\text{Number of Incident Particles}} \]

\[ N_A = n_{gas} \cdot S \cdot C \] where C is the ratio of target concentration

Values reported: \(10^{-5} \leq S \leq 10^3\)

Typical values: \(0.1 \leq S \leq 10\)

Note: Sputtering is a complex process with low/mid/high energy ranges with individual mechanisms each. → We will approach it phenomenologically

\[ S \propto \rho_n \cdot \delta_n(E) \cdot \lambda_s \]

where:

  • \(\rho_n\): atomic density
  • \(\delta_n(E)\): energy loss in target
  • \(\lambda_s\): (surface) binding energy
