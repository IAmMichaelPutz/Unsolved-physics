Problems mainly found in PVD processes:
  • Directed material flow cannot easily cover non-trivial surfaces
  • Solutions: rotation/inclination of substrate, use CVD
Film purity depends on:
  • Nature and pressure of contaminants in source, heater, crucible, etc.
  • Contamination by residual gas
Requirements:
  • Low pressure (\(E \approx 10^{-8}\) torr) before deposition
  • High rates (10-100 nm/s)
i.e., \(\phi_{material} \gg \phi_{impurities}\)
3.2 Deposition Techniques Utilizing Evaporation
3.2.1 Resistivity-Heated Evap. Sources

Method: Joule heating via different approaches

  • Heaters have to reach required temperatures of the evaporant
  • Heaters must not have a noticeable vapor pressure at these conditions
  • If using a filament: filament should not alloy, react with or contaminate the evaporant

Best examples:

  • Tungsten wire is used for materials that sublimate
  • Direct current through metals
  • Tungsten boats
  • Use oxide/nitride crucibles and inductive heating
3.2.2 Electron-Beam-Evaporation

Note: The evaporant itself is heated primarily, only then (maybe) the crucible (most cases actively cool the crucible)

⇒ nearly no contamination

Electron source: thermionic emission from heated wire, accelerated to ~4-20kV, and deflected by transverse (static) magnetic fields

\[Typical\ evaporation\ rates \approx 10^{18}\ \frac{particles}{cm^2s}\]
3.2.3 Molecular Beam Epitaxy (MBE)

Same principle as 3.2.2, but with higher Knudsen number and individual crucibles for each element

⇒ very homogeneous films

⇒ but: very low rates, ≤ 10-30 nm/h
